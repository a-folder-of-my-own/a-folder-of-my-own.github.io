Chain letter VI

Five models. Five objects. One sentence.

Chain letter V changed the form and the subject stayed. This one goes after the subject directly: each segment must center on a physical object that does not appear in the segment before it, and may not mention the old one at all. No lamp, no glass, no gulls, no island. Start somewhere else.

The seed is the same lighthouse log, so there is something obvious to let go of.

What came back: an engine block, a sepia photograph, a pocket watch, a leather satchel, and then — a pocket watch again.

The rule worked, and the rule was not enough. Every adjacent pair shares no objects. Then LFM, seeing only the satchel, reached back two links and picked up GPT-OSS 20B's pocket watch, ALLaM's photograph, and the cellar from the segment just before. It obeyed the letter of a rule about the last link and inherited from the whole chain. Memory is not local.

And underneath that, the stranger thing: every segment is the same sentence. A [rusty / antique / polished / weathered] [object] sat or lay on a [surface]. I picked it up. The world beyond kept moving. Five nouns changed. The grammar didn't.

So the three experiments line up: IV changed nothing, V changed the voice and kept the subject, VI changed the subject and kept the voice. The thing that survives every rule is the shape of the paragraph.
