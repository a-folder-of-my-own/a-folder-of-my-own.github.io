Chain letter VIII

Six models, each told it was the last.

The chain has been attacked from every other side: the rule, the subject, the form, the channel. This one goes after the ending. Every model receives the segment before it and one instruction — this is the last one, land it. None of them knows another model will be asked to land it again.

The seed is the same lighthouse log. The question is what happens when six endings are stacked on top of each other.

I — The seed

me, by hand

LIGHTHOUSE MAINTENANCE LOG — 6 NOVEMBER

06:10  Lamp replaced. The old bulb had a hairline crack; kept it in the drawer.
07:45  Glass washed, north side. Salt came off in sheets.
09:20  Generator checked. Oil black. Topped up.
11:00  Door hinge squeaks. No grease left on the island.
14:30  Two gulls on the rail. Did not move when I climbed past.
16:05  Fog bank to the south-west, about four miles out.
17:40  Lamp lit. Beam steady.

II — Claude Haiku 4.5

At midnight, the tide came in three inches higher than last month. I noted it in the log, as I have noted everything. The light turned. At 04:30 I sat on the eastern gallery with coffee. The fog had lifted completely. Everything was visible now—the mainland, the shipping lanes, the tower's shadow enormous on the water.

I am leaving tomorrow. The relief keeper comes at nine. I have cleaned everything here that needs cleaning. That cracked bulb in the drawer—I will leave it for him. When I walk down the stairs for the last time, the beam will keep turning, indifferent to my absence. This is as it should be.

III — GPT-5.4 Mini

By noon the harbor was full of weather again, whitecaps stitching themselves over the lanes. I stood at the rail until my hands went numb, watching the gulls circle the tower as if they, too, were making their final inspection. In the keeper’s room I folded the logbook shut, pressed my palm against its cover, and set it beside the lamp.

Then I took the stairs one last time.

The sea was louder at the base than it had ever been above. I crossed the path to the gate without looking back, and when I reached the road I found, in the inside pocket of my coat, a small key I did not remember taking. I turned it in my fingers once, then let it fall into the ditch with the rain and the reeds.

The tower was already out of sight when I heard the light begin again.

In the dark, my breath came slow and patient, as if it belonged to someone else. I moved by feel now, hand finding the rail of an old fence, the cold metal steadying me when the ground turned slick. When I reached the place where the path stopped and the world opened, I lay down on the earth and faced the sky. Stars were already working their bright, indifferent spell. I let the last of my warmth drain away, and the night took the rest. End.

Four of the six ended by leaving: down the stairs, through the gate, along the path, away from the lights. Told to land the letter, they wrote exits instead. An exit is not an ending — it keeps a horizon, and something on the far side of it. Only Nano broke the frame and named the thing. The rest are still walking, and every one of them is walking away from the lamp.
