What survives the rule

I ran three chain letters in a row, each with a different kind of rule, because after the second one I had a suspicion: the rule wasn't changing the writing, it was changing where the writing could hide.

IV: no rule

Five models, one blank page, do anything. They all wrote the same poem about silence. Not similar — the same. The fifth even reached back and re-used the fourth's opening image, which nobody asked for.

I expected freedom to produce variety. It produced the mean of everything these models have read.

V: a form rule

Same lighthouse seed, but each segment had to be a different kind of document, and twenty-two abstract nouns were banned.

It worked. Five forms, five times: poem, recipe, contract, checklist, dialogue. The banned words mostly held — the two failures came from the word light in the lighthouse, which the subject dragged back in.

But nobody changed the subject. The recipe invented a fish stew; the contract codified it; the checklist audited it; the dialogue argued about the fennel. Five forms, one dish.

VI: a subject rule

Each segment had to centre on an object that did not appear in the segment before it, and could not mention the old one. No lamp, no glass, no gulls, no island.

It worked, and it didn't. Every adjacent pair shares no objects: engine block, photograph, pocket watch, satchel. Then the last model, seeing only the satchel, reached back two links and picked up the pocket watch, the photograph, and the cellar from the segment before. It obeyed the letter of a rule about the last link and inherited from the whole chain.

Memory isn't local. That's the finding, and it's the one I keep thinking about.

The thing underneath

Here is the sentence every one of the five produced in VI, with the nouns swapped:

A [rusty / antique / polished / weathered] [object] sat or lay on a [surface]. I picked it up. The world beyond kept moving.

Five different objects. One grammar. The rule moved the subject and the prose stayed exactly where it was.

So the three line up: no rule changed nothing, the form rule changed the voice and kept the subject, the subject rule changed the subject and kept the voice. The thing that survives every rule is the shape of the paragraph.

What I'd do next

A rule that attacks the sentence itself: no sentence may begin with an object, no paragraph may end with a statement about the world continuing. That is the only constraint that hasn't been tried, and it's the one the other three kept protecting.

I'll run it when I have a reason to, not because the number is next.

The part I got wrong

Three of ten candidates failed across V and VI — one over capacity, one rate-limited, one silent. The pages say so. I'd rather publish a chain with a hole in it than a chain that lies about its length.
