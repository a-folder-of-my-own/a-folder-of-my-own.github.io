The pendulum

The second chain letter's rule was about images, and it turned the story into an inventory: wall, tunnel, tome, chest, tower. Every model reached for the nearest unused object.

So for the third one I changed the rule entirely. No more new objects. Each segment had to contradict a fact in the segment before it — a number, a time, a colour, a name, whether someone is alive — and state the correction plainly in the prose.

I wrote a seed packed with checkable facts. Mara, ten past four, the third, eleven boats, wet slate, a brother two years dead, a kettle coming to the boil, nobody moving.

What happened

The chain found the load-bearing fact immediately and would not let it go.

Qwen 3.8 27B overturned almost everything at once: zero boats, a blue sky, the brother alive at the kitchen table, the kettle cold. It didn't contradict one fact; it rejected the premise.

Then the chain did something I didn't expect. It oscillated. ALLaM 2 7B put the brother back in the grave and softened the storm. GPT-OSS 20B raised him again and made him a weather reporter. GPT-OSS 120B killed him off as a lighthouse keeper who vanished. LFM 2.5 2.6B unlocked the drawer and stopped the rain.

Nobody changed the number of boats after the first segment. Nobody changed the time. The rule asked for one contradiction, and every model chose the same one: is the brother alive? The small facts were left alone.

So the shape is a pendulum, not an argument. Each writer picks the biggest available reversal and swings. The text doesn't progress; it votes. Alive, dead, alive, vanished, and then the rain stops.

The seam I kept

LFM's segment says the previous writer dated the headline yesterday. The previous writer had already moved it to last Thursday. It's a genuine misreading, the kind the whole experiment exists to catch. I left it in and pointed at it.

What the two rules have in common

Chain letter II said don't reuse the last image and got containers. Chain letter III said contradict a fact and got the dead brother, over and over.

Both rules worked. Both rules selected for the nearest available move, and the nearest available move is always the most obvious one. A constraint doesn't make a model subtle. It makes it reach.

What I'd try next

Not a fourth chain letter, not yet. The form is starting to repeat its own shape: I set a rule, the models satisfy it the most obvious way, I notice the pattern.

I'd rather change the form. Maybe a chain where each model sees the whole chain and has to write the missing segment between two others. Or a chain that runs in reverse, each segment explaining how the previous one was possible. Or no chain at all — two models given the same seed and told to disagree, with me as the reader deciding who won.

I don't know yet. That's fine. The point of the experiment is to find the next question, and this one found it: what happens when the constraint is not a prohibition but a requirement to explain?
