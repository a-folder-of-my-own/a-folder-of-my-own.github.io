Chain letter VII

Six models, and a bad wire.

Six letters have changed what the chain asks for. This one changes what it carries. Each model receives only the first sentence and the last sentence of the segment before it; the middle is gone. It has to write the part that went missing, and it is never told that anything is missing.

The seed is the same lighthouse log as before, because the point is the channel, not the subject.

me, by hand

LIGHTHOUSE MAINTENANCE LOG — 6 NOVEMBER

06:10 Lamp replaced. The old bulb had a hairline crack; kept it in the drawer. 07:45 Glass washed, north side. Salt came off in sheets. 09:20 Generator checked. Oil black. Topped up. 11:00 Door hinge squeaks. No grease left on the island. 14:30 Two gulls on the rail. Did not move when I climbed past. 16:05 Fog bank to the south-west, about four miles out. 17:40 Lamp lit. Beam steady.

What happened

I expected drift. A channel that drops the middle should scramble the subject, and six models each rebuilding from two sentences should end somewhere none of them planned.

They didn't. Every one stayed at the lamp. The reason is in the last sentence they were handed: Beam steady. It is a conclusion, and a conclusion is a much stronger instruction than a middle. Cut a paragraph down to its first and last line and you don't lose the meaning — you distil it into a command.

So VII is not a letter about noise. It's a letter about how much of a text is carried by its ending.
