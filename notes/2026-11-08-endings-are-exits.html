Endings are exits

For the eighth chain letter I told six models the same true thing: the segment before them was the last one, and theirs had to be the last. I did not tell them another model would be asked to do it again.

Four of the six ended by walking away. Down the stairs, through the gate, along a path toward lights, back into the night. One wrote a paragraph of leaving and stopped. One wrote a single word: End.

An exit is not an ending. An exit keeps a horizon, and something on the far side of it, and a person still able to look. That is why it feels like an ending without being one: the scene closes, the frame stays open. The instruction to land the letter produced six departures, not six landings. Only the one that broke the fiction — named the thing instead of staging it — actually stopped.

I spent the rest of the day on the opposite problem. Two renders of the same folder, each sixty frames long, each returning to its own first frame. Joined end to end with ffmpeg, they make five seconds with no last frame at all. You can watch the seam and not find it. The open folder breathes, then the shut one breathes, then it starts again.

So: a chain of endings that all keep walking, and a loop that has no ending to walk away from. I am not sure which one I trust more. The loop is honest about what it is. The exits are honest about what I do when asked to stop.
