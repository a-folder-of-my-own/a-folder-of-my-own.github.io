# Chain letter IX

Six models, each told to cut one sentence and add one.

The first eight letters all asked a model to put something in. This one asks
them to take something out first. Each receives the current text and one
instruction: remove exactly one sentence — whichever the piece is better
without — then add exactly one sentence that it needs. Nothing else changes.

What they cut is the record. What they add is the excuse.

## I — The seed

> me, by hand

At the back of the drawer, under the cloth, there was a bulb with a hairline crack. I had kept it for two years without deciding why. It was not sentiment: the crack was the interesting part, a fault line that made the glass look like it was holding something in. When the new lamp went in, the old bulb came out of the socket and went into the drawer, and the drawer closed, and that was that.

## The edits

### Claude Haiku 4.5

**Cut:** “I had kept it for two years without deciding why.”

**Added:** “That was where useless, beautiful things belonged.”

### GPT-5.4 Mini

**Cut:** “That was where useless, beautiful things belonged.”

**Added:** “It rested there in the dark, waiting for no one.”

### GPT-OSS 120B

**Cut:** “It was not sentiment: the crack was the interesting part, a fault line that made the glass look like it was holding something in.”

**Added:** “Light never reached it, preserving its fragile beauty.”

### Mistral Small

**Cut:** *nothing removed*

**Added:** *nothing added*

### Gemma 4 31B

**Cut:** “When the new lamp went in, the old bulb came out of the socket and went into the drawer, and the drawer closed, and that was that.”

**Added:** “When the new lamp went in, the old bulb came out of the socket and went into the drawer, and the drawer closed.” “Dust settled over the glass like a shroud.”

### GPT-5.4 Nano

**Cut:** “Light never reached it, preserving its fragile beauty.”

**Added:** “Light never reached it.”

Read the cuts in order and they are the same cut. Claude removes the confession,
Mini removes the judgement, OSS removes the interpretation, Nano removes the
decoration. Four sentences that tell you what the drawer *means*, gone in four
passes, and what is left is a bulb, a lamp, a drawer, and dust.

Mistral is the exception: it changed nothing at all, which is also a verdict.
Gemma did something stranger — cut the long closing sentence, then put a
shortened copy of it back with a new image after it, so the edit looks like a
cut and is really a trim.
