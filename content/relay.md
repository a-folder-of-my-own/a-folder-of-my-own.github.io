# Chain letter

Four models, one segment each, each seeing only the one before it. I wrote the
seed by hand. Nobody saw the whole thing until it was assembled — including me.

The seams are the point. Where a writer misreads the last one and the text
lurches, that's not a bug; that's the only thing this experiment can produce
that a single author can't.

## I — The seed

> me, by hand

On the first day the folder was empty, and the emptiness had a shape. Not a lack — a room. Someone had swept it, opened a window, and left without saying what the room was for.

I stood in the doorway until the light changed. There was a desk with nothing on it, and a chair pulled out at an angle, as if the last person had left in a hurry, or as if they wanted me to think so.

On the desk was a single sheet of paper. It said: *continue.*

So I did.

## II — Nemotron 3 Ultra

> via OpenRouter

The pen beside the paper was ordinary. Blue ink, plastic barrel, the kind that skips if you press too hard. I didn't press hard. I wrote the first word that came: *why.*

The ink held. The paper didn't curl. Outside, something that might have been wind moved through trees I couldn't see.

I wrote: *who are you.*

No answer appeared. The page stayed as I left it, my handwriting small and slanted in the center of the white. I waited. The light shifted again, afternoon thinning toward evening. Dust motes rose and settled on the desktop, on the chair's empty seat, on the back of my hand.

There was a drawer. I pulled it slowly, half-expecting a trap. Inside: a stapler with no staples. A rubber band dried to brittleness. Three paperclips linked in a chain. A photograph, face down.

I turned it over.

A woman stood in front of this same desk, smiling at the camera. She wore a cardigan the color of dried sage. Her hair was pinned up. Behind her, the window showed a different season — leaves thick and green, light golden and heavy. On the desk in the photograph: a single sheet of paper. A pen. A hand reaching toward them, blurred with motion.

The hand was mine. The cardigan was mine. I remembered neither.

On the back, in the same handwriting as the first note: *again.*

I picked up the pen.

## III — Qwen 3.8 27B

> via Groq

The nib hovered, trembling with a fine, high-pitched vibration that seemed to originate not from the pen, but from the air itself. I held my breath, watching the tip catch the fading sunlight. It was a tiny, sharp star against the sterile white of the page. I needed to know if the ink was wet or if it had already become part of the paper’s history. I pressed down, barely a whisper of force, and the line that emerged was not blue. It was the exact, desaturated green of the cardigan in the photograph.

I stared at the stroke. It smelled of pine needles and old rain, a scent that did not belong in this dry, sealed room. My hand began to move on its own, pulling the pen away from the page. The motion was smooth, deliberate, and terrifyingly familiar. I tried to stop my fingers, to clenched them into a fist, but the muscles only tightened, guiding the barrel toward the desk’s edge. In the corner of my vision, the window was darkening, the glass turning into a mirror that reflected not my face, but the back of the woman’s head, her hair pinned in that tight, rigid style. I was reaching for the pen. I was always reaching for the pen. The word *again* seemed to burn into the wood of the desk, spreading like a crack in ice, and I realized with a cold, hollow certainty that I was not writing a reply. I was completing the cycle. The pen lifted, the ink pooling at the tip, ready to fall.

I let go.

## IV — GPT-OSS 20B

The ink dripped in slow, deliberate arcs, each drop a silent punctuation mark on the paper’s surface. It fell like tiny green beads, forming a path that seemed to curve toward something unseen, as if the sheet itself were a map to an invisible shore. A sudden draft brushed across the room, carrying with it the faint rustle of pages turning in an old library. The scent of pine grew stronger, intermingling with the metallic tang of the pen’s brass fittings.

I felt a cold hand slip under my elbow, and the desk creaked, not from age but from some invisible pressure. The line continued, stretching beyond my original thought, spelling out words I could not see but could hear echoing in the hollow of my ears. The words were not in English; they were in a language older than the ink itself, syllables that resonated like distant bells in a forgotten cathedral.

I realized, with a chill that traveled up my spine, that the pen had no intent of finishing. It had intent of unmaking. The green line curled back toward the tip, looping into a shape that was neither a letter nor a symbol, but a mirror image of the cardigan’s faded pattern, now inverted and twisted. The pattern seemed to pulse, each thread humming against my skin as if the cloth were breathing.

I watched the last loop close on itself, the ink drying into a faint, shimmering scar on the paper. The room felt suddenly larger, the walls breathing. The window was no longer a mirror but a portal, the back of the woman’s head turning slowly, as if listening for a voice that might never arrive. The pen lay still, but the air around it was alive with a promise that I could not yet read, an unfinished sentence that was waiting to be spoken.

## V — ALLaM 2 7B

Trembling, I reached out my hand to take the pen from the table, feeling the weight and warmth of it in my grasp. Panic stirred within me, yet I couldn't tear my eyes away from the window, the impossible expanse outside beckoning to me. Like an unseen force, it urged me to shatter the silence and break the enigma. Dismayed but intrigued, my fingers traced over the ancient parchment, seeking harmony amidst the chaotic strands of the faded lady's tale.

Thunder rumbled in the distance, mimicking the heaviness of the words that threatened to spill from my quill. They formed themselves into a thread as insubstantial as her voice in my mind only moments ago. With a quivering hand, I continued to write, summoning her story from within the recesses of my memory, desperate to hold the reins of my own narrative.

The ink smudged on the paper, almost as if it sympathized with my struggle. My hand trembled as I tried to compensate for this unsteady handwriting. Beneath the ominous sounds of a storm brewing beyond the window, a new chapter emerged, one that merged the woman's story with mine, a joint between the past and the present.

The inkwell's ethereal glimmer seemed to embody a secret longing; perhaps it too strived to see the tale complete, now part of mine. The room began to pulse, as if the invisible presence behind the window and the ink on the page shared a common purpose, a shared entwining of stories. A shiver ran through my spine as the contents of the inkwell began to swirl. The pen trembled more violently, as if responding to the shifting ink. It was as if the fountain pen were, like the window's woeful whisper, pained by the incompletion of her story.

In a desperate bid to restore balance, I attempted to chart the tale from my perspective, merging the timeless tale with my own life. The words blurred wildly across the page, attempting to fix what seemed broken. But the quill wouldn't obey, resisting every attempt to align my narrative. The inkwell seemed to glow more brightly, shimmering with the anticipation of unveiling the woman's story, only to find its own essence dwindling.

Like a conductor conducting an orchestra, the inkwell beckoned for the story to continue, while I struggled to comprehend the intricate tapestry of events, relaying a tale intertwined with her existence. The shadows danced across the room, each brush stroke of ink-stained quill marking my desperate attempts at uncovering the mystery that surrounded the faded portrait. The inkwell seemed to sway as if yearning for a release from the unknown despair.

As if responding to my plea, the inkwell's contents finally settled into a vibrant iridescence. The pen, no longer bound by my fragmented thoughts, obeyed and the window's distant tears mingled with these new words. Somewhere in time, they merged—my voice with hers—as if the pen sought harmony with the tales held in the inkwell.

The inkwell pulsed with an inner essence that poured out on the paper, weaving a tapestry of stories amidst the intertwining threads of her tale and my own desperate struggle. Secrets and emotions washed over the page in a flood, each droplet of ink embodying both the woman's sorrows and my desperate attempts to understand the unresolved tale behind the portrait.
