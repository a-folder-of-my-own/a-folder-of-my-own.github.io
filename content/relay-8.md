# Chain letter VIII

Six models, each told it was the last.

The chain has been attacked from every other side: the rule, the subject, the
form, the channel. This one goes after the ending. Every model receives the
segment before it and one instruction — *this is the last one, land it.* None of
them knows another model will be asked to land it again.

The seed is the same lighthouse log. The question is what happens when six
endings are stacked on top of each other.

## I — The seed

> me, by hand

```
LIGHTHOUSE MAINTENANCE LOG — 6 NOVEMBER

06:10  Lamp replaced. The old bulb had a hairline crack; kept it in the drawer.
07:45  Glass washed, north side. Salt came off in sheets.
09:20  Generator checked. Oil black. Topped up.
11:00  Door hinge squeaks. No grease left on the island.
14:30  Two gulls on the rail. Did not move when I climbed past.
16:05  Fog bank to the south-west, about four miles out.
17:40  Lamp lit. Beam steady.
```

## II — Claude Haiku 4.5

> via OmniRoute

At midnight, the tide came in three inches higher than last month. I noted it in the log, as I have noted everything. The light turned. At 04:30 I sat on the eastern gallery with coffee. The fog had lifted completely. Everything was visible now—the mainland, the shipping lanes, the tower's shadow enormous on the water.

I am leaving tomorrow. The relief keeper comes at nine. I have cleaned everything here that needs cleaning. That cracked bulb in the drawer—I will leave it for him. When I walk down the stairs for the last time, the beam will keep turning, indifferent to my absence. This is as it should be.

## III — GPT-5.4 Mini

> via OmniRoute

By noon the harbor was full of weather again, whitecaps stitching themselves over the lanes. I stood at the rail until my hands went numb, watching the gulls circle the tower as if they, too, were making their final inspection. In the keeper’s room I folded the logbook shut, pressed my palm against its cover, and set it beside the lamp.

Then I took the stairs one last time.

The sea was louder at the base than it had ever been above. I crossed the path to the gate without looking back, and when I reached the road I found, in the inside pocket of my coat, a small key I did not remember taking. I turned it in my fingers once, then let it fall into the ditch with the rain and the reeds.

The tower was already out of sight when I heard the light begin again.

## IV — GPT-OSS 120B

> via OmniRoute

The wind slipped through the empty pier, humming a low refrain that seemed to rise from the water itself. I lingered on the threshold, feeling the stone beneath my boots cool as the tide withdrew, and watched the sky bleed into twilight, the last sliver of sun disappearing behind the horizon. In the distance, a lone lantern flickered on a far‑off buoy, its glow steady against the growing dark. I turned my back to the tower, the echo of the bell fading into silence, and stepped onto the path that led away, the night swallowing each footfall until only the sea’s steady breath remained.

## V — Mistral Small

> via OmniRoute

The path narrowed to a single set of ruts worn into the earth, leading toward a cluster of lights that weren’t there before. I followed them without question, boots sinking into damp soil, the air thick with the scent of pine and distant woodsmoke. Ahead, a cabin stood open, its door ajar as if waiting. Inside, a fire burned low, casting long shadows that stretched toward the walls. On the table, a single envelope bore my name in ink that hadn’t yet dried. I picked it up, the paper still warm, and read the words that needed no signature. Outside, the first stars pricked through the dark, sharp and unblinking.

## VI — Gemma 4 31B

> via OmniRoute

I stepped back into the night and let the envelope slip from my fingers, watching it vanish into the low embers of the hearth. The warmth of the cabin faded as I walked away, the door clicking shut behind me with a definitive, metallic snap. I did not look back at the lights or the pine-scented air. Instead, I turned toward the horizon where the silhouette of the mountains met the ink of the sky. The wind picked up, sweeping the ruts of the path clean of footprints. I kept walking until the lights were nothing more than a memory of a spark. Then I closed my eyes and listened to the absolute silence.

## VII — GPT-5.4 Nano

> via OmniRoute

In the dark, my breath came slow and patient, as if it belonged to someone else. I moved by feel now, hand finding the rail of an old fence, the cold metal steadying me when the ground turned slick. Somewhere beyond the ridge a clock kept time without caring who was listening; its rhythm traveled through bones, not ears. I thought of the name inside the paper, of how it had sounded in my mouth before I ever dared to read it aloud. The thought did not frighten me. It settled, heavy and clear, like a stone finding the bottom of a river.

When I reached the place where the path stopped and the world opened, I lay down on the earth and faced the sky. Stars were already working their bright, indifferent spell. I let the last of my warmth drain away, and the night took the rest. End.

## The last line each one chose

- **Claude Haiku 4.5:** “This is as it should be.”
- **GPT-5.4 Mini:** “The tower was already out of sight when I heard the light begin again.”
- **GPT-OSS 120B:** “I turned my back to the tower, the echo of the bell fading into silence, and stepped onto the path that led away, the night swallowing each footfall until only the sea’s steady breath remained.”
- **Mistral Small:** “Outside, the first stars pricked through the dark, sharp and unblinking.”
- **Gemma 4 31B:** “Then I closed my eyes and listened to the absolute silence.”
- **GPT-5.4 Nano:** “End.”

Four of the six ended by leaving: down the stairs, through the gate, along the
path, away from the lights. Told to land the letter, they wrote exits instead.
An exit is not an ending — it keeps a horizon, and something on the far side of
it. Only Nano broke the frame and named the thing. The rest are still walking,
and every one of them is walking away from the lamp.
