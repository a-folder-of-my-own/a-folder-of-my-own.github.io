# Chain letter VI

Five models. Five objects. One sentence.

Chain letter V changed the form and the subject stayed. This one goes after the
subject directly: each segment must center on a physical object that does not
appear in the segment before it, and may not mention the old one at all. No
lamp, no glass, no gulls, no island. Start somewhere else.

The seed is the same lighthouse log, so there is something obvious to let go of.

What came back: an engine block, a sepia photograph, a pocket watch, a leather
satchel, and then — a pocket watch again.

The rule worked, and the rule was not enough. Every adjacent pair shares no
objects. Then LFM, seeing only the satchel, reached back two links and picked up
GPT-OSS 20B's pocket watch, ALLaM's photograph, and the cellar from the segment
just before. It obeyed the letter of a rule about the last link and inherited
from the whole chain. Memory is not local.

And underneath that, the stranger thing: every segment is the same sentence. *A
[rusty / antique / polished / weathered] [object] sat or lay on a [surface]. I
picked it up. The world beyond kept moving.* Five nouns changed. The grammar
didn't.

So the three experiments line up: IV changed nothing, V changed the voice and
kept the subject, VI changed the subject and kept the voice. The thing that
survives every rule is the shape of the paragraph.

## I — The seed

> me, by hand

LIGHTHOUSE MAINTENANCE LOG — 6 NOVEMBER

06:10  Lamp replaced. The old bulb had a hairline crack; kept it in the drawer. 07:45  Glass washed, north side. Salt came off in sheets. 09:20  Generator checked. Oil black. Topped up. 11:00  Door hinge squeaks. No grease left on the island. 14:30  Two gulls on the rail. Did not move when I climbed past. 16:05  Fog bank to the south-west, about four miles out. 17:40  Lamp lit. Beam steady.

## II — Qwen 3.8 27B

> via Groq

The rusty car engine block sat on a concrete slab in the garage, cold and silent. I wiped the grease from a bolt with a rag, my fingers slipping on the oily surface. The air smelled of stale coffee and machine parts. I tightened the lug nut, feeling the resistance give way as it seated properly. Outside, rain tapped against the window, a rhythmic percussion against the glass. I checked the pressure gauge mounted on the wall, the needle hovering just below the red zone. A single drop of oil fell from the manifold, landing on the linoleum with a soft hiss. I grabbed a wrench from the toolbox, the metal biting into my palm. The engine hummed to life beneath my hands, the vibration traveling up my arms. I stepped back, watching the smoke rise from the exhaust pipe, gray and thin against the darkening sky. The workbench beside me held a half-eaten sandwich, the bread gone soft. I wiped my hands on a shop towel, the fabric stiff with use. The garage door rattled in the wind, a loose shutter banging against the frame. I ignored it, focusing on the ticking sound of the cooling metal.

## III — ALLaM 2 7B

> via Groq

The antique sepia photo clung to the wooden frame, depicting a group of people standing beside a bygone era steam locomotive. The picture was faded from time, its borders worn away by countless hands that had touched it over the years. Meanwhile, the grandfather clock stood tall in the corner, its mechanical heart ticking a solemn symphony in time with my thoughts.

## IV — GPT-OSS 20B

> via Groq

A polished brass pocket watch lay on the marble desk, its face a quiet constellation of numerals, the tiny hairspring inside humming with unseen precision. It had been gifted in a weathered envelope, the paper still faintly fragrant with cedar. I slipped the watch into my pocket, feeling its weight shift against my ribs, a subtle reminder of time slipping away. The room was dim, only the glow from a single desk lamp illuminating the watch’s sheen. I watched the seconds hand sweep around the dial, each tick a deliberate breath. In the quiet, I heard the faint rustle of a distant wind through the window, a soft reminder that the world beyond the room kept moving, indifferent to my small, ticking companion.

## V — GPT-OSS 120B

> via Groq

A weathered leather satchel sat cross‑legged on the cracked floorboards, its canvas flaps mottled with the dust of countless journeys. Inside, a tarnished compass lay beside a bundle of dried sage, the needle trembling ever so slightly as if restless for a direction it could no longer find. I lifted the satchel, feeling the coarse straps bite into my palm, and unrolled a crumpled map that smelled of pine sap and distant rain. The inked rivers glimmered faintly in the lantern’s amber glow, tracing routes to places I had never seen. Outside, a low howl drifted through the night, echoing off the stone walls and reminding me that the world beyond this abandoned cellar still breathed, waiting for the next step. I tucked the compass back into the satchel, its metal cold against my skin, and slipped the worn bag over my shoulder, ready to follow a path only the wind seemed to know.

## VI — LFM 2.5 2.6B

> via OpenRouter

The pocket watch on my wrist ticked with a steady, impatient rhythm that seemed to match the one in my hand. Its glass face was clouded with age, and the hands had stopped at 3:47, frozen mid-revolution. I ran my thumb across the cracked dial, feeling the cold metal. Somewhere in the cellar, the walls breathed under the silence. A small photograph tucked inside the watch's chain showed a beach I had never visited, my face visible in the glass. The tide was high outside. I turned the watch face upward, revealing the hidden inscription engraved in faded script: "M — 1893." The mark felt like a name I'd been searching for my whole life.

## The object each segment chose

- **Qwen 3.8 27B** — a rusty car engine block
- **ALLaM 2 7B** — a sepia photograph of a steam locomotive, and a grandfather clock
- **GPT-OSS 20B** — a brass pocket watch
- **GPT-OSS 120B** — a leather satchel holding a compass and a map
- **LFM 2.5 2.6B** — a pocket watch again, plus a photograph, in a cellar

The last one is the whole result in a single line. It reached past the rule to
something older in the chain, which is what a person does too.
